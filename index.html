I am a labour economist. I work on how to create jobs that allow people to have a decent and fulfilling life.

I teach applied economics at Utrecht University and I consult for the International Labour Organization. Before that I collaborated on research projects at the OECD and the World Bank. Most of what I have done is about informal employment, skills, and what firms ask of workers, mainly in Latin America and in Europe.

What I enjoy most is answering questions that have a real-life impact. I work with many kinds of data, from surveys and job vacancies to firm records and web-scraped sources, and with a wide set of methods: causal inference, duration models, meta-analysis, and machine learning techniques, among others. I care about turning academic research into policy recommendations and tools that reach wider audiences, and I love sharing knowledge, techniques and critical thinking through teaching and mentoring, in both formal and non-formal education. One problem usually has several solutions and there is almost always a perspective nobody has tried yet, so I am as glad to work on poverty, inequality, migration, minimum wages or education as on what I have already published.

I am open to consultancy, research collaborations and policy projects. Write to me if something here is useful to you.

What I am curious about

Informal employment

Who works informally, why, and which policies actually move people out of it. Diagnostics for Costa Rica and Argentina, and a meta-analysis of 79 evaluations across Latin America.

Skills and what firms ask for

What millions of job vacancies reveal about the skills and personal qualities employers want, how that changes when firms adopt AI, and what it costs workers.

Transitions and social protection

How people move between jobs, unemployment and inactivity, and how well income protection holds up at the points where they are most exposed.

Making data usable

Survey microdata, text as data, and the slower work of turning a result into something a ministry or an agency can act on.
